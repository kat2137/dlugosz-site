## The problem

A bra is a static object fitted to a body that is not static. Breast volume
changes across the hormonal cycle, tissue stiffness changes with it, and the load
on the supporting ligaments changes hour by hour with posture and activity. Every
woman I interviewed described the same mismatch in different words — needing two
sets of bras, or accepting pain for part of every month.

The structure makes it worse. Breast tissue is a mix of adipose, fibrous and
glandular material with elastic moduli that differ by three orders of magnitude,
suspended from the chest wall by ligaments that can only take a few percent
strain before damage accumulates.

> Inadequate support is as damaging as excessive tension. Ligament strain occurs
> both from overstretching and from insufficient support, and the optimal support
> level changes across the cycle and throughout the day based on tissue stiffness,
> volume, and activity.

## Constraint

Support has to be adjustable after the garment is made, by the wearer, without
tools — and it has to hold that adjustment. Elastic cannot do this. It is a
material chosen for a property it then loses, over months, while the wearer keeps
paying for the garment.

I measured what the alternatives actually offer. Bonded lycra came in at 44,600
N/m along the selvedge; polyester neoprene at 7,300; a biodegradable polyamide at
5,800. The stiffest fabric is not the most supportive garment — it is the one
that dictates a single fixed compression. Choosing the softest and adding
adjustable structure inside it is the opposite approach.

## What I built

<Video
  id="D4LJ3_pYS1M"

Air, rather than elastic. Two fabric layers with sealed silicone channels between
them, inflated and vented to change the compression of the surface — the
principle of a McKibben muscle, flattened into a textile.

The channel geometry is a space-filling curve, so one inlet reaches a large area
without a manifold of separate tubes to leak at every junction. Channels are cast
in PlatSil Gel 25 — skin-safe and food-grade — against a mould modelled in Fusion
360, bonded to a fabric base, and fed through a luer lock.

The channel layout changes by use case. For the hormonal cycle, channels across
the pectoral fascia and under the bust absorb volume change. For shape
adaptation, side and front pads take up partial volume differences and an
inflatable underwire stabilises the base. For activity, a removable panel adds
chest-wall stability and a side channel reduces lateral bounce.

Control is a pump, a solenoid valve and an MPRLS pressure sensor reading
continuously, so the garment notices lost rigidity rather than waiting for the
wearer to. Both actuators are switched by MOSFETs with flyback diodes, off a
shared 6 V supply, with the sensor on I²C.

Three modes: one raises pressure, one releases it, and one matches the current
cycle day against the pressure the wearer chose on that same day last cycle. The
board advertises itself over mDNS, so it is reachable from any phone on the same
network — no account, no cloud, nothing leaving the house.

The whole garment is designed to come apart at end of life: the fabric is 100%
biodegradable polyamide, and the silicone separates from it for reuse.
