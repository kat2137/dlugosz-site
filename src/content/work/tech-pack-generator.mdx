## The problem

Bills of materials and stitch breakdowns are repetitive work that needs no design
judgement, and a general model gets the vocabulary wrong — confusing lining
colour with main fabric colour, or naming stitches the way an amateur would.

## Constraint

Production companies need rules and jargon they already recognise. An open
model invents plausible-sounding terms, mislabels stitches the way an amateur
would, and confuses lining colour with main fabric colour — all of which cost
time to correct downstream.

A fixed taxonomy fixes three things at once: stitch and hardware names stay in
factory vocabulary, materials map to a known set rather than a guess, and
translations stay consistent across every pack, because the terms are bounded
before anything is generated.
